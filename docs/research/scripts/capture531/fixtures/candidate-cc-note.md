- Findings from outside sources go on the research shelf. When you read documentation, a changelog, a paper, a benchmark or a web page and find something a later investigation of the same subject would need (a limit, a behaviour, a trade-off or a limitation of a named system), store it at once: `memcue add -shelf research -source tool -keywords "<words>" -- "<finding>. Source: <document, version or date>."`. Store what the source states, not your conclusion for the task; say so when it is only a vendor's or an anonymous claim, and leave out what nothing supports. Search the shelf first (`memcue search -in shelf:research "<words>"`) and correct a fact that says the same or an older version instead of adding a second one.
- Ordinary search leaves the shelf out, and an answer with facts does not say that the shelf has more. When a task returns to a system, library or question that may have been researched before, also run `memcue search -in shelf:research "<words>"`. A shelf fact is a lead from an earlier reading: check its source and date against the material at hand before relying on it, and correct or retire it when it no longer holds.
