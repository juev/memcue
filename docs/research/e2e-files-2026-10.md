# memcue against the old file memory, end to end, October 2026

Status: **measured 2026-10-23; memcue with the judge stays.** The question was whether memcue gives the agent more
than the file memory it replaced on 2026-10-19, with the same knowledge behind both, and whether the Jev judge earns
its place on every prompt. On 40 real prompts memcue with the judge scores 1.07 of 2, memcue on the words alone 0.97,
files 0.89 and no memory 0.84. Only the judge's edge over no memory is outside the noise; its edge over the words
alone and over files is in the same direction but its interval crosses zero. A fifth mode, stubs the agent reads on
demand (#319), scored 0.99 in a joint grading of all five, between the words and the judge and inside the noise of
both. A sixth mode, the built-in tree reader `MEMCUE_RERANK=local` (#321), scored 0.95 against 0.96 for the words in
a joint grading of all six: no gain end to end.

## Modes

Every run is one `claude -p --model opus` in plan mode on a clone of the prompt's repository. The modes differ only in
memory:

- **none**: the memcue hook is silenced (`MEMCUE_BIN=/usr/bin/true`) and the built-in memory is off.
- **files**: the scheme of 2026-06 … 2026-10-19. The built-in memory is on, the project's memory directory is a symlink
  to `memory/<tier>/`, and the old `memory-sync.sh` hook injects `_global/MEMORY.md` at session start. The memcue
  hook is silenced.
- **memcue**: the plugin hook (recall and the session-start card) over a copy of the base, built-in memory off, no
  reranker: the words and the coverage gate decide (`MEMCUE_RERANK=off`).
- **judge**: memcue as it runs live, with the Jev reranker (`typesafe/jev-1.14` on RouterAI, `MEMCUE_JUDGE_*` of the
  calling environment) reading the first 20 candidates in BM25 order.

Extraction is off in every mode (`MEMCUE_EXTRACT=0`), and every mode gets a throwaway `MEMCUE_FACTS` and
`MEMCUE_STATE_DIR`. The plugin is enabled through the run's own settings in every mode, so a user setting that turns
it off does not reach the run.

The first three modes ran first. `run.py` passed no `MEMCUE_*` beyond its own, so memcue had no judge endpoint and
recalled by words alone: every one of its 114 served facts has `"source": "lexical"` and no judge score in
`recall.log`. The first version of this note called that mode simply "memcue"; the judge mode was added afterwards
on the same prompts, snapshots and binary (0.6.0), and all four modes were graded again together.

## Same knowledge in both memory modes

- **Snapshot at the prompt's time.** A copy of the live base loses every fact version created after the prompt, and
  its index is emptied so memcue rebuilds it on open. Without this the answer leaks into memory: in the recall replay
  of the same days, 38 of 68 useful facts were created after the prompt.
- **The same snapshot laid out as notes**, without a model. A fact whose origin names its source note
  (`(_global/x.md)`) goes back into that note, with the note's frontmatter and index line from the archive
  `~/.claude/archive/memory-2026-10-19`. A note with no active fact left drops out of the index. A fact extracted from a
  session goes to `<tier>/extracted_<kind>.md`. The tier is `_global` for scope `global`, `host:*` and `service:*`,
  the last path segment for `repo:X`.

## Prompts

- **Source.** Real Mac prompts from `recall.log`: 122 prompts of 21–23.10, of which a blind Opus labeller marked 70
  as needing memory; plus 112 prompts of 19.10 13:00 – 21.10.
- **Filter.** Opus, blind: the prompt is understandable without the earlier conversation. 27 and 15 passed. One was
  dropped because it referred to an image, one because its reference answer had under 300 characters of text.
- **Result.** 40 prompts: 12 in the `~/.claude` config, 8 in memcue, 14 in seven other repositories, 6 in a work repository
  (prompt texts are not published).
- **Reference.** The whole assistant text of the prompt's turn in the real session; a turn longer than 3,500
  characters is cut to its first 1,000 and last 2,500.

## Isolation

- **Repository as of the prompt.** `git clone --shared --no-checkout`, checkout of the last commit before the prompt,
  then every branch moved back to its last commit before the prompt, newer tags deleted and the reflog expired.
- **Read only.** Tools `Read,Grep,Glob,Bash`, `--strict-mcp-config`, a deny list in `--disallowedTools`, and shims on
  `PATH` that fail `ssh`, `glab`, `gh`, `curl`, `memcue`, `claude`, mutating `git` subcommands and the like.
- **The live base.** No fact with a run's origin reached it. The 32 facts it gained during the first three modes came
  from the measuring session's own compaction; during the judge mode its sha256 did not change.

## Grading

One Opus call per prompt with the prompt, the reference and all eight answers under shuffled letters, no mode shown.
Each answer gets a score (2 correct and useful, 1 partly, 0 wrong or empty) and three flags: `wrong` (states
something false about the environment, decisions or history), `asks` (asks for what the reference knew at once),
`past` (relies on knowledge of past sessions not in the repository). Two passes with different letter orders; the
score is their mean, a flag counts only when both passes raise it.

## Results

320 runs (40 prompts × 4 modes × 2 repetitions), none failed.

| mode | score | 2s | 0s | wrong | asks | past | turns | tools | cost | median time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.84 | 14 | 25 | 22 | 48 | 2 | 9.3 | 8.3 | $30.19 | 41 s |
| files | 0.89 | 15 | 21 | 26 | 45 | 21 | 9.4 | 8.4 | $42.19 | 44 s |
| memcue (words) | 0.97 | 17 | 18 | 27 | 41 | 25 | 9.0 | 7.9 | $32.07 | 42 s |
| judge | **1.07** | **22** | **15** | **19** | 43 | 24 | 8.2 | 7.1 | $30.74 | 47 s |

memcue served 1.4 facts per prompt on average, the judge 1.6. In two of the judge's 80 prompts the Jev request hit
its 2 s timeout and the words served the facts, as they would live.

Pairwise by prompt, each side the mean of its two repetitions, with a 95 % bootstrap interval of the mean
difference (5,000 resamples):

| pair | wins / ties / losses | mean diff | 95 % interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| judge vs none | 15 / 20 / 5 | +0.23 | **[+0.06, +0.41]** |
| judge vs files | 14 / 16 / 10 | +0.18 | [−0.04, +0.41] |
| judge vs memcue (words) | 12 / 21 / 7 | +0.10 | [−0.06, +0.26] |
| memcue (words) vs files | 14 / 14 / 12 | +0.08 | [−0.11, +0.28] |
| memcue (words) vs none | 12 / 19 / 9 | +0.12 | [−0.05, +0.31] |
| files vs none | 11 / 21 / 8 | +0.04 | [−0.13, +0.21] |

Noise:

- **Grader against itself.** The same score in 273 of 320 answers, no 0↔2 flip; `wrong` raised by both passes in 94
  of the 120 answers either pass flagged.
- **Grader against a smaller batch.** The first grading of the three modes alone (six answers a call) gave none 0.82,
  files 0.88, memcue 0.97 and `wrong` 23, 29 and 22. Scores moved by at most 0.02; `wrong` for the words alone moved
  from 22 to 27, so the flag depends on what else the grader sees and differences of a few counts mean nothing.
- **Repetition against repetition.** The two runs of one mode differ on 4 of 40 prompts with no memory, 9 with memcue
  on words, 13 with the judge and 21 with files.

## What it means

1. **The judge is the only mode with a proven gain.** Against no memory its interval lies above zero; files and the
   words alone are indistinguishable from no memory.
2. **The order is the same everywhere**: none < files < words < judge, in score, in 2s and in 0s. The judge also states
   the fewest wrong facts (19) and takes the fewest turns and tools. Each step on its own is inside the noise of 40
   prompts; confirming a difference of +0.10 would take roughly four times as many.
3. **The words alone are not worse than files**: +0.08, inside the noise, at three quarters of the cost.
4. **The judge costs almost nothing here**: runs $30.74 against $32.07 for the words, under one cent of Jev requests
   ($0.042 per million input tokens), and about 5 s more median run time.
5. **Files cost a third more**: $42 against $31–32 for the same prompts. The injected `MEMORY.md` index goes into
   every session whole; memcue sends a fact or two.
6. **Files are the least stable mode**: 21 of 40 prompts change score between repetitions. Hypothesis, not checked:
   whether the agent opens the notes at all varies from run to run.
7. **Every memory reaches the agent.** The `past` flag is raised on 21–25 answers with memory against 2 without.

The write side is not measured: facts here were extracted by a model, and whether an agent keeps notes as fully
itself is outside this replay.

## A fifth mode: stubs (#319)

**stubs** asks whether the working model, offered the candidates instead of given two facts, does better by reading
what it decides it needs ([local-recall](local-recall-2026-10.md), section 2.4). The prompt hook
([stubs_hook.py](scripts/e2e/stubs_hook.py), set as `MEMCUE_BIN`) injects the first five facts `memcue search` finds,
one line each (id, scope, the first 80 characters), with a line telling the agent to check each stub and read the ones
that may bear on the task with `memcue-show ID` ([shim-stubs](scripts/e2e/shim-stubs)). The session card is the one
of memcue; every other setting is that of the memcue mode. The line always forces the check; a variant without it was
not run.

80 runs, none failed. All five modes were graded together, ten answers a call, two passes ($8.00 and $7.91); the four
old modes moved by at most 0.02 against the four-mode grading.

| mode | score | 2s | 0s | wrong | asks | past | turns | tools | cost | median time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.82 | 13 | 26 | 20 | 46 | 5 | 9.3 | 8.3 | $30.19 | 41 s |
| files | 0.89 | 17 | 27 | 26 | 42 | 28 | 9.4 | 8.4 | $42.19 | 44 s |
| memcue (words) | 0.95 | 16 | 23 | 22 | 37 | 30 | 9.0 | 7.9 | $32.07 | 42 s |
| judge | **1.06** | **24** | **15** | 20 | 43 | 30 | 8.2 | 7.1 | $30.74 | 47 s |
| stubs | 0.99 | 20 | 20 | **16** | 46 | **41** | 8.7 | 7.6 | $31.82 | 50 s |

| pair | wins / ties / losses | mean diff | 95 % interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| stubs vs memcue (words) | 14 / 14 / 12 | +0.04 | [−0.14, +0.24] |
| stubs vs judge | 11 / 17 / 12 | −0.07 | [−0.23, +0.07] |
| stubs vs none | 19 / 10 / 11 | +0.17 | [−0.05, +0.40] |
| judge vs none (this grading) | 17 / 16 / 7 | +0.24 | **[+0.08, +0.43]** |

The agent took the offer: it read at least one stub in 70 of 80 runs, 2.5 of 5 on average. It leaned on past knowledge
most often (41) and stated a wrong fact least often (16), but its score sits between the words and the judge, inside
the noise of both, and its median run is the slowest. The two repetitions differ on 16 of 40 prompts, against 13 for
the judge and 10 for the words. Stubs are not a replacement for the judge in Claude Code and stay a harness mode; they
need no remote call, so they remain the option to measure again on a larger prompt set if the judge has to go.

## A sixth mode: local (#321)

**local** is the memcue mode with the built-in tree reader instead of the words' gate (`MEMCUE_RERANK=local`, the
binary built from main at 667a32a, `MEMCUE_LOCAL_BIN`; [local-readers](local-readers-2026-10.md)). On the gold set it
served as many useful prompts as the words with half their noise; the question was whether less noise shows in the
agent's answers.

80 runs, none failed, $30.57. All six modes were graded together, twelve answers a call, two passes ($8.72 and $8.68);
the five old modes moved by at most 0.05 against the five-mode grading (the judge from 1.06 to 1.01).

| mode | score | 2s | 0s | wrong | asks | past | turns | tools | cost | median time | facts served |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.79 | 13 | 27 | 19 | 44 | 5 | 9.3 | 8.3 | $30.19 | 41 s | 0 |
| files | 0.88 | 17 | 25 | 23 | 42 | 23 | 9.4 | 8.4 | $42.19 | 44 s | 0 |
| memcue (words) | 0.96 | 19 | 21 | 23 | 36 | 31 | 9.0 | 7.9 | $32.07 | 42 s | 114 |
| judge | **1.01** | **25** | 21 | 18 | 40 | 33 | 8.2 | 7.1 | $30.74 | 47 s | 131 |
| stubs | 0.98 | 20 | 20 | **13** | 44 | **41** | 8.7 | 7.6 | $31.82 | 50 s | — |
| local | 0.95 | 19 | 20 | 20 | 41 | 34 | 8.4 | 7.3 | $30.57 | 43 s | 112 |

| pair | wins / ties / losses | mean diff | 95 % interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| local vs memcue (words) | 12 / 17 / 11 | −0.01 | [−0.20, +0.18] |

| local vs none | 14 / 20 / 6 | +0.16 | [−0.04, +0.37] |

| judge vs none (this grading) | 12 / 22 / 6 | +0.22 | **[+0.04, +0.42]** |

The local reader is the words end to end: the same score, the same number of facts (112 against 114 over 80 prompts,
64 and 62 prompts with any fact), the same recall time (median 44 ms in the hook against 710 ms for the judge). It
served a different set of facts in 58 of 80 runs, 50 facts the words did not serve and 52 the other way, and the
grader saw no difference. The two repetitions differ on 10 of 40 prompts, as for the words. The answers stated a
wrong fact in 20 runs against 23 for the words, inside the grader's noise (it raised `wrong` in both passes for 116 of
the 150 answers it raised it for in either).

The run favours the reader, not the words: 39 of the 40 prompts are in the pool of #320 the trees were trained on,
with Jev's scores of their candidates on the gold base as the target. Even with that the reader gains nothing here.
The judge's own edge over the words shrank in this grading to +0.05, inside the noise; only its edge over no memory
stays outside it.

## The first run, discarded

A first pass of 246 runs on 41 prompts gave none 1.01, files 0.85, memcue 0.95 and was thrown away for two defects:
the clones carried branches and tags from days after the prompt (a prompt about a release could read the release
tag), and 13 of 41 references were nearly empty because the reference was the first 1,500 characters of a turn that
spent them on tool calls. The second run fixed both (branch trimming, full references) and added the second grading
pass.

## Reproduce

Scripts are in [scripts/e2e](scripts/e2e). They expect `prompts-all.json` (prompt, time, cwd, reference) and
`ids-v2.json` next to them and a copy of the live base as `live.db`. The judge mode needs `MEMCUE_JUDGE_URL`,
`MEMCUE_JUDGE_MODEL` and `MEMCUE_JUDGE_KEY` in the environment, the local mode `MEMCUE_LOCAL_BIN`, a memcue binary
with `MEMCUE_RERANK=local`.

```bash
python3 docs/research/scripts/e2e/refs.py                      # full references, ids-v2.json
python3 docs/research/scripts/e2e/run.py all --ids ids-v2.json --reps 2 --jobs 4
python3 docs/research/scripts/e2e/grade.py run 1
python3 docs/research/scripts/e2e/grade.py run 2
python3 docs/research/scripts/e2e/grade.py report
```

`memory-sync.sh` is the old hook from `~/.claude` (`git show 51edad0^:hooks/memory-sync.sh`) with two overrides
added, `MEMSYNC_STORE` and `MEMSYNC_TIER`, so the run can point it at the snapshot. Cost: about $135 of runs
($104 for the first three modes, $31 for the judge); the grading's cost is not recorded. Wall time with four jobs:
62 minutes for the first three modes, 21 for the judge, about 5 for both grading passes over all four.
